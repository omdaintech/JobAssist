Amplify Growth Network

Role: Founder Timeline: 2.5 Years to Scale Status: Founder Exit - Company Still Operating
500K€ Annual Revenue at Exit
2000+ Influencers
4000+ Affiliates
1B+ Combined Reach

The Challenge

The influencer marketing space was fundamentally broken. Brands were overpaying mega-influencers with questionable ROI. Small creators had no access to brand partnerships. No efficient marketplace existed connecting the two sides.

The market needed a solution that could serve both sides: give brands access to authentic, cost-effective influencers, and give small creators access to legitimate brand opportunities.

The Strategy

I identified a critical market insight: nano and small influencers deliver better ROI than mega-influencers. They have authentic audiences, lower costs, and higher engagement rates. The opportunity was to build a two-sided marketplace with a dual business model.

Business Model:

  • SaaS for Affiliates: 20% of affiliate earnings through self-service platform
  • Agency for Influencers: 15-20% commission on managed campaigns
  • Platform Mechanics: Brands launch campaigns, affiliates/influencers pick offers, track earnings in real-time

The strategy was to build network effects: more influencers attract more brands, more brands attract more influencers, creating a sustainable flywheel.

Execution

I led the company through three distinct growth phases:

Phase 1 (0-6 months): Foundation

Recruited initial influencer and affiliate network directly. Focused on quality over scale. Built platform infrastructure and established operational processes.

Phase 2 (6-12 months): Validation

Landed early brand clients and proved the model worked. Refined platform based on feedback. Established pricing and commission structures. Built credibility through successful campaigns.

Phase 3 (1-2.5 years): Scale

Tier-one brands began approaching us directly. Network effects kicked in. Scaled to 2000+ influencers and 4000+ affiliates. Secured major clients: H&M, Amazon, Noon, Zara, Defacto. Reached 500K€ annual revenue.

Impact

  • Built company from zero to 500K€ annual revenue
  • Created sustainable dual-revenue model (SaaS + Agency)
  • Secured tier-one global brand clients
  • Achieved network effects and inbound demand
  • Successfully exited - company continues operating under new leadership
  • Demonstrated marketplace dynamics and platform business model expertise

Why It Matters

This case demonstrates founder-scale strategic thinking. I identified a market gap, designed a differentiated business model, built a two-sided marketplace from scratch, scaled to profitability, created network effects, and balanced growth with revenue generation.

The success wasn't just in building a company—it was in understanding market dynamics, designing sustainable economics, executing across multiple disciplines (product, sales, operations), and creating a transferable business that continues operating successfully after my exit.

Mini Perfumes

Role: Market Entry Consultant / Launch Lead Market: UAE → Sweden Timeline: 2 Months
2x Target Exceeded
1,000 Orders in 2 Months
30 Retail Partnerships
250-400K SEK First 2 Months Revenue

The Challenge

A UAE-based fragrance brand wanted to enter the Swedish market with zero local presence, no distribution infrastructure, and no brand awareness. The goal was ambitious: acquire 500 customers in the first quarter.

The market entry needed to be fast, cost-effective, and sustainable—not just a one-time launch spike, but the foundation for ongoing growth.

The Strategy

I designed an integrated launch approach rather than relying on a single channel. The strategy combined multiple disciplines toward one aligned outcome:

  • Brand Positioning: Adapted brand messaging and naming for Swedish market
  • E-commerce Infrastructure: Built optimized online store with SEO and conversion focus
  • Retail Partnership Strategy: Established relationships with 30 retail partners before launch
  • Content & Social: Created video content and social media presence
  • Coordinated Launch: Went live across all channels simultaneously to create momentum

The key insight: coordinated execution across all touchpoints creates more impact than sequential, siloed launches.

Execution

I led a small team through structured planning and coordinated execution. Rather than launching one channel, waiting for results, then starting the next, we orchestrated everything together:

Pre-Launch (Weeks 1-4): Brand positioning work, e-commerce setup, retail partnership outreach, content production, and marketing preparation all happening in parallel.

Launch (Day 1): E-commerce site live, retail partnerships announced, social media active, content distributed—all coordinated for maximum impact.

Post-Launch (Weeks 5-8): Optimization based on data, scaling what worked, maintaining retail partnerships for sustained visibility.

Impact

  • 1,000 orders in first 2 months (2x the 500 customer target)
  • 250-400K SEK revenue in first 60 days
  • 30 retail partnerships established and ongoing
  • Sustainable distribution infrastructure built
  • Brand presence established in new market
  • Retail partnerships continue generating visibility and sales

Why It Matters

This case demonstrates the power of integrated strategy over siloed execution. Marketing without distribution fails. E-commerce without partnerships limits reach. Content without conversion infrastructure wastes awareness.

By coordinating all elements—positioning, e-commerce, partnerships, content—toward one aligned goal, we exceeded targets by 2x and built sustainable infrastructure, not just a temporary launch spike.

Sawarly

Role: Business Development Manager Market: MENA (Middle East & North Africa) Timeline: 2.5 Years
40% Client Growth
Only 4K Provider in MENA
Enterprise Facebook, Twitter, FIFA
Acquired Successful Exit

The Challenge

Sawarly was a media coverage company serving live events across MENA. Then COVID hit. Events were cancelled. The entire business model froze overnight. Competitors went silent or shut down. We had weeks to pivot or collapse.

The challenge wasn't just survival—it was identifying where the market was moving and repositioning the company to lead in the new environment.

The Strategy

I identified a critical market gap: events were moving online and needed high-quality live-streaming. But no MENA provider offered 4K quality across all social platforms simultaneously.

The Solution: Strategic partnership with Eventus by Bevvy, a technology platform provider.

  • Technology Integration: Offer Eventus's 4K streaming platform
  • Reseller Model: Sawarly becomes local expert and sales force in MENA
  • Complete Repositioning: From "media coverage company" to "4K SaaS provider"
  • Market Timing: Only provider offering this capability during COVID transition

Execution

I led the partnership negotiation, business model restructuring, and client acquisition:

Partnership Development: Negotiated and structured the Eventus partnership, defining terms, responsibilities, and go-to-market approach.

Business Model Pivot: Repositioned company positioning, pricing, sales approach, and service delivery around 4K SaaS offering.

Enterprise Sales: Secured major clients including Facebook, Twitter, CAF (Confederation of African Football), and FIFA.

Geographic Expansion: Established UAE operations to serve growing demand.

Operational Excellence: Built seamless workflow for multi-platform launches and client delivery.

Impact

  • 40% client portfolio growth during COVID (while competitors shut down)
  • Market leadership: Only 4K streaming provider in MENA
  • Enterprise clients: Facebook, Twitter, CAF, FIFA
  • Geographic expansion: UAE operations established
  • Company acquired - successful exit event
  • Transformed crisis into competitive advantage

Why It Matters

This case demonstrates strategic thinking under crisis. It wasn't just about closing deals—it was about recognizing a fundamental market shift and making a bold pivot that required partnership development, business model transformation, and rapid execution.

The partnership didn't just save the company; it created a more valuable business model that led to acquisition. This separates survivors from leaders: the ability to see where markets are moving and position ahead of the shift.
